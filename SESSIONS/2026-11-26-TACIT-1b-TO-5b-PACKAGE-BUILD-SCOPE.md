### Physical quantity contracts and engineering boundaries

Before executing an electrical calculation, document the distinction between AC and DC, single-phase and three-phase, RMS and instantaneous values, line-to-line and line-to-neutral voltage, current per conductor and circuit current, real and apparent power, and the difference between power factor and efficiency. For AC calculations, distinguish resistance from impedance and preserve reactance or phase-angle assumptions where the producer model uses them.

Use a declared canonical unit internally only where the source contract permits conversion. Document which conversions are performed by the adapter and which are intrinsic to the existing function. Prevent a caller supplying resistance per kilometre to a port expecting total circuit resistance. Specify whether length is one-way route length, conductor length, loop length or some other defined measure. For parallel circuits or conductors, expose the actual supported model rather than silently applying a factor.

Resistive loss and energy-loss functions are particularly vulnerable to false precision. An annual estimate derived from constant load over a supplied number of hours is not a time-series energy model. A calculation based on a single resistance value is not proof of conductor temperature, cable ampacity or installation conditions. State whether resistance is at a reference temperature, actual operating temperature, or supplied by the caller. If resistance variation, skin effect, proximity effect, dielectric loss, sheath losses, thermal environment or harmonic content are outside the source model, make that clear. Do not add engineering assumptions by stealth merely to make an example run.

Never make the package issue automatic selection of a cable size, installation approval, protection setting or an energisation recommendation without the appropriate verified engineering model and separate competent review. Do not use a synthetic fixture to imply that a real project has a particular available capacity or circuit rating. The software may provide a calculation with stated assumptions; the consumer remains responsible for the adequacy of those assumptions and applicable engineering standards.
